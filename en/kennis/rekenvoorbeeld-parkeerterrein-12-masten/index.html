Worked example: car park with 12 columns of 6 metres

A fictional car park of 100 by 60 metres needs 20 lux from twelve steel lighting columns of 6 metres. We work out step by step how much light is required, which VIA street luminaires fit and what the complete bill of materials costs, indicatively.

The starting points

The site in this example is fictional but realistic: a car park of 100 by 60 metres (6,000 m²) next to a company building, in use until late evening, with lorry traffic at the loading docks. The client asks for an average illuminance of 20 lux, the guide value EN 12464-2 gives for car parks with heavy traffic. A quiet car park needs only 5 to 10 lux.

Fixed choices: twelve conical steel TURRIS columns of 6 metres in RAL 9005, VIA street luminaires on the 60 mm spigot and one NEXUS column cut-out with fuses per column. All prices exclude VAT; Lichtplein supplies businesses only.

Step 1: how many lumens are needed

The rule of thumb for an open area starts with area times required illuminance: 6,000 m² × 20 lux = 120,000 lumens that must actually land on the surface.

Two corrections follow. Part of the light falls outside the area or is lost in the optic; for a street luminaire with a wide road optic at 6 metres we use a utilisation factor of 0.5 in this example. The luminaire also gets dirty and its output drops slightly over the years: maintenance factor 0.8. Installed, you therefore need 120,000 / (0.5 × 0.8) = 300,000 lumens, or 25,000 lumens per column with twelve columns.

Step 2: the luminaires

The VIA range runs from 25 to 100 W at up to 160 lm/W, IP66, with 10 kV surge protection. Three variants side by side, each with the same factor of 0.4 (utilisation times maintenance):

  • 1 × 50 W per column (LP-VI112, 20 to 50 W set by dip switch, 6,750 lm): 12 × 6,750 = 81,000 lm installed, about 5 lux average. Suitable for an overflow car park.
  • 1 × 100 W per column (about 15,000 lm): 180,000 lm, about 12 lux. Meets the 10 lux for medium traffic.
  • 2 × 100 W per column on a double bracket: 360,000 lm, about 24 lux. This is the variant that reaches the required 20 lux with a margin for soiling.

For this example we choose the third variant: 24 luminaires of 100 W, 2,400 W in total. Choose the version with DALI-2 or AstroDIM if the site may dim to 50 per cent after midnight; the extra cost of the driver can then pay for itself through lower consumption.

Step 3: column positions and uniformity

Twelve columns on 6,000 m² means 500 m² per column. A logical layout is three rows of four: the rows at 10, 30 and 50 metres from the long side, the columns at 12.5 / 37.5 / 62.5 / 87.5 metres. The 25 metre spacing is a good four times the mounting height, which we treat as the practical upper limit for a road optic at 6 metres.

That is exactly why a rule of thumb is not enough. The average is right, but the uniformity Uo (minimum divided by average) must be at least 0.25 for a car park, and only a calculation proves that. For 350 euros excluding VAT Lichtplein produces one via /en/lichtplan/: you receive an isolux plot, the tilt angle per luminaire and a bill of materials that can be ordered straight away. Glare towards the entrance and neighbouring properties can also be included in the calculation.

Step 4: columns, cut-outs and fuses

The 6 metre TURRIS column is conical, made of S235JR steel, hot-dip galvanised and, for this project, additionally powder coated in RAL 9005 (jet black). It is supplied to the Dutch standard, with a door opening for the cut-out and a 60 mm spigot on which the VIA luminaire clamps with adjustable tilt.

A NEXUS column cut-out goes behind the door. With two luminaires per column you choose the version for two fuses, so each luminaire is protected separately: two porcelain E27/DII screw caps, two fuse links and an earth braid to the column.

Electrically: 24 × 100 W = 2,400 W, a good 10 A. Spread the three rows over three phases and switch via a photocell or an astronomical time switch in the distribution board.

Complete bill of materials

  • 12 × TURRIS conical steel lighting column 6 m, RAL 9005
  • 24 × VIA LED street luminaire 100 W, IP66, optic as per the lighting calculation (with DALI-2 or AstroDIM as preferred)
  • 12 × double bracket for two luminaires on one column (price on request)
  • 12 × NEXUS column cut-out for 2 fuses
  • 24 × porcelain E27/DII screw cap
  • 24 × DII fuse link
  • 12 × earth braid
  • 1 × lighting calculation with isolux plot and bill of materials

Not in the bill of materials, but in your budget: foundations, underground cable, groundworks, the distribution board and installation. Lichtplein does not supply these.

  • Columns: 12 TURRIS steel columns of 6 metres in RAL 9005, sharp price on request, answer within 48 hours

Frequently asked questions

Does a taller column save luminaires?

A greater mounting height mainly improves uniformity, not the amount of light: you still need about 300,000 lumens. We supply TURRIS in 4 and 6 metres; other heights we request for you via /en/offerte/.

Why 20 lux and not 10?

EN 12464-2 gives guide values of 5 lux for light traffic, 10 lux for medium and 20 lux for heavy traffic. On a site with lorries, loading docks and shift work 20 lux is usual; a visitor car park needs only 10 lux.

Can I switch from 50 to 100 W later?

Yes, the 60 mm spigot and the cut-out stay the same. Choose the two-fuse cut-out straight away, so a second luminaire can be connected later without a new cut-out.

What do I need to supply for the lighting calculation?

A site plan, a screenshot of the aerial photo or just the address, plus the required lux level and the column height. You receive a proposal with positions, lux values and bill of materials.

Are the fuses included with the cut-out?

The NEXUS range lists cut-outs, screw caps, fuse links and earth braids as separate items; we put the set per column together in the quotation.
