Sassenheim: comfortable light without glare

In a municipal street in Sassenheim, a plainly styled street luminaire with two independent light sources delivers roughly 1,500 to 15,000 lumens. Why that simplicity keeps glare low, and how you achieve the same with the adjustable street luminaires of VIA.

The street in Sassenheim

Sassenheim, in the municipality of Teylingen, has the residential streets typical of a Dutch village: cars, cyclists and pedestrians share the same space. The columns are low, at 4 to 6 metres, the house fronts close to the road. A luminaire at 4 metres hangs almost in a cyclist's line of sight: glare is a real risk.

When the lighting was renewed, the choice fell on a street luminaire with a clean, simple housing: no decorative canopy or protruding lens, just a flat light window that directs the light downwards and along the road. Lichtplein was not involved in this project. We describe it because the design choices behind it apply directly to luminaires from our range.

Two light sources, one housing

The luminaire works with two independent light sources. Each source has its own optic, chosen from several light distributions, and its own lumen output. Together they deliver roughly 1,500 to 15,000 lumens, depending on the setting. The same housing therefore suits a footway with one source at low output as well as a through road with two sources at full output.

One luminaire type for the whole neighbourhood, then, with its own setting on every column.

Why the shape limits glare

Glare on the street comes from light reaching the eye at a shallow angle. EN 13201 limits it with intensity classes (G classes) for the luminous intensity at 70, 80 and 90 degrees. A flat optic mounted horizontally meets the stricter classes almost by itself: no light above the horizon, little light towards the road user.

A luminaire without a domed cover or translucent side walls emits only through its light window. With a warm colour temperature of 2700 or 3000 K you see the road, not the lamp.

How you achieve the same with VIA

The street luminaires of VIA follow the same principle: a flat housing, a Type II or Type III optic and an output you set per luminaire. The series runs from 25 to 100 W; on the dip switch versions you select the output on site. That gives roughly 1,500 to 15,000 lumens depending on the version, with an efficacy of up to 160 lm/W: the same range as in Sassenheim.

  • Footway or narrow residential street: 25 W, 1,500 lm, Type II optic, mounting height 4 metres.
  • Through road in a neighbourhood: 50 to 100 W, Type III optic, mounting height 6 metres.
  • All versions: IP66, 10 kV surge protection, tilting on a 60 mm spigot, 5 year guarantee.

Set the tilt angle to 0 degrees: every degree upwards raises the luminous intensity in the glare zone and the light entering bedrooms. For dimming back after midnight, choose the version with DALI-2 and D4i or a driver with AstroDIM.

Lighting level and columns

Residential streets are usually assigned class P3 or P4 from EN 13201: an average of 7.5 or 5 lux with a minimum of 1.5 or 1 lux. At 4 metres, 25 to 40 W per lighting point is often enough for P4, depending on column spacing; a lighting calculation confirms the uniformity in advance. For the columns, the conical steel columns of TURRIS in 4 and 6 metres fit, with a NEXUS cut-out (column connection box) in the door opening.

Frequently asked questions

Does VIA also have two light sources in one luminaire?

No, VIA works with one adjustable light source per luminaire. You cover the same range of roughly 1,500 to 15,000 lumens by choosing the right version and setting its output; the series runs from 25 to 100 W. At junctions you can combine two luminaires on one column.

How do I avoid glare on a 4 metre column?

Mount the luminaire horizontally (0 degrees), choose a flat optic with no upward light and keep the output modest: 25 to 40 W is usually enough at 4 metres. A warm colour temperature of 2700 K helps as well.

Which optic suits a residential street?

Type II for narrow streets and footways with the column close to the kerb, Type III for wider carriageways. VIA offers both.

Can I change the output later?

Yes. On the dip switch versions you change the output on site inside the luminaire; on the DALI-2 versions you adjust the dimming profile from the control system.
