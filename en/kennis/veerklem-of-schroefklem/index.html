Spring clamp or screw terminal in wiring accessories: which to choose

A switch or socket outlet is connected with either a spring clamp or a screw terminal. That difference decides how fast you install, which conductor you can use and how the connection holds up over the years. Here are the pros and cons and which Lichtplein range uses which system.

Two ways to secure a conductor

Behind every switch, dimmer and socket outlet sits the same task: a copper conductor has to press against a live part with sufficient contact pressure for years. With a screw terminal you tighten a screw that clamps the conductor directly or via a pressure plate. With a spring clamp (also called a push-in terminal) you push the stripped conductor into an opening, after which a steel spring presses it against the busbar.

Both systems are standardised and safe, provided you use them for what they are designed for. The difference lies in installation time, the type of conductor, releasing the wire and how the connection behaves over the years.

Installation speed: where the gain is

With a spring clamp the connection takes two actions: strip to the indicated length and push in. No screwdriver, no tightening, no doubt about whether the screw is really tight. With a screw terminal you add loosening, inserting and tightening for each conductor, plus a brief pull test.

That saves a few seconds per connection, but it adds up. A worked example: a house with 60 switch and socket points and an average of three conductors per point comes to 180 connections. At 10 seconds saved per conductor that is half an hour per house, and 20 hours across a project of 40 houses.

The second gain is in checking. A spring clamp is either right or wrong: the conductor is pushed in to the stop or it is not. With a screw terminal the quality depends on the tightening torque, and that is not always consistent at the end of a working day.

Reliability: contact pressure over the years

Copper deforms slowly under pressure. A screw terminal that was tight on installation can gain a little play after years of heating and cooling: the contact pressure drops, the contact resistance rises and the joint runs warmer. That is why retightening screw terminals in distribution boards is a well-known maintenance item. Behind a switch in a flush box that hardly ever happens, so there the installation has to be right first time.

A spring clamp maintains its own contact pressure: the spring follows the conductor as it settles. Spring clamps are therefore not very sensitive to vibration and temperature changes and do not need retightening. A properly tightened screw terminal on solid conductor also lasts for decades (apply the tightening torque specified by the manufacturer, usually below 1 Nm for wiring accessories), but a spring clamp leaves less room for an installation error.

Solid or flexible conductor

This is the most important practical difference. A push-in terminal is designed for solid conductor: the single-core 1.5 or 2.5 mm² wire that is drawn into conduit in Dutch housing. A solid conductor is stiff enough to push the spring open and then stays straight in the clamp.

Flexible conductor (fine-stranded, as in flex cords) cannot simply be pushed into a push-in terminal: the fine strands splay out and the spring grips them poorly. Flexible conductor only works with a ferrule, and only if the terminal datasheet permits it. A screw terminal accepts solid and flexible conductor without further ado, although a ferrule on fine-stranded copper is the neat solution there too.

Rule of thumb: housing and renovation with solid single-core wire, choose spring clamps with confidence. If you work a lot with flexible conductor, for example surface-mounted installations with flex, a screw terminal or a spring clamp with ferrules is more convenient.

Releasing and reuse

A screw terminal can be loosened and retightened as often as you like; the conductor goes back in unchanged. With a spring clamp you release the conductor through the release opening: press the spring in with a small screwdriver and pull the conductor out.

The clamped part of the conductor usually shows a slight impression of the spring afterwards. Cut off the end and strip it again before reinserting. That costs a few centimetres of wire, so allow enough length in the flush box. In a refurbishment where switches are moved several times, a screw terminal is slightly more forgiving.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use flexible conductor in the LUXA spring clamp?

Only with a ferrule and only if the datasheet of the item concerned permits it. Solid single-core 1.5 and 2.5 mm² wire goes straight in.

Do I need to retighten screw terminals behind a switch?

In practice that hardly ever happens, because the plate is not opened again. So apply the correct tightening torque on installation and give each conductor a brief pull test.

How much insulation do I strip for a spring clamp?

Follow the strip length indicated on the product or in the datasheet, usually 10 to 12 mm for spring clamps. Too short gives poor contact, too long leaves bare copper outside the clamp.

Can I combine LUXA and PRIMA in one project?

Yes, that is common: PRIMA in plant rooms and storerooms, LUXA in visible spaces. Keep to one range per room, because cover frames and appearance differ between ranges.
