Flaneerkade, Den Helder: custom work with the municipal logo in the luminaire

On the Flaneerkade in Den Helder stands a lighting column whose luminaire carries the municipal logo: a custom shield, designed by an urban planner. What such a detail demands of luminaire, column and coastal durability, and how you request comparable custom work from Lichtplein.

A waterfront promenade in the naval city

Den Helder, the naval city at the northern tip of North Holland, gained a waterfront promenade with the Flaneerkade. Instead of a standard catalogue column, it has a slender lighting column with a luminaire in which the municipal logo is incorporated as a shield: a lighting point that belongs to the place.

The shield was designed by an urban planner; the lighting supplier engineered it into a manufacturable, watertight component. A sketch on paper thus became a recognisable lighting point on the quay.

How a logo shield in a luminaire works

A custom shield is usually a laser-cut plate of aluminium or steel, mounted in or on the housing, outside the optic. The logo then appears as a silhouette against the lit surface or as an illuminated outline on the outside. Two things must remain intact:

  • The light distribution. The shield must not cover the lens, otherwise the lighting calculation no longer holds.
  • The sealing. Every additional seam is a risk to the IP rating. Outdoors on the coast, IP66 is the minimum.

What the coastal location demands

Den Helder lies by the sea, in the most severe wind zone of the Netherlands and in a salt-laden atmosphere. That places demands on column, luminaire and fixings:

  • Column to EN 40, calculated for the local wind load.
  • Hot-dip galvanised steel with powder coating, or aluminium, which does not rust. On a salty coast you allow for a high corrosivity category; ask for a coating system matched to it.
  • Stainless fixings, including for the shield itself.
  • A driver with 10 kV surge protection: long cable runs along open water pick up spikes.

The light level on a promenade

A promenade with many pedestrians in the evening falls within the pedestrian classes (P classes) of the standard for public lighting. As a guide value, in practice you aim for 7.5 to 10 lux average, with good uniformity and without glare, so that faces remain recognisable at a distance. Warm white of 2700 to 3000 K suits a waterfront amenity area and contains less blue light than 4000 K, which is often a requirement near water and greenery because of the effect on nocturnal wildlife. A lighting calculation on the actual column spacing gives certainty about the light level.

Frequently asked questions

Can any luminaire be given a logo?

No. The shield must sit outside the optic and leave the housing sealed. On a flat street luminaire the logo can go on the side or underside of the housing, on a post-top luminaire in the canopy or the column head. Always ask for a drawing before you order.

How many columns are needed for custom work?

Project prices for columns apply from 20 units. A custom shield is assessed per project: we tell you per request what is feasible and in which quantities.

Which column do you choose on the coast?

Aluminium does not rust and is a logical choice by the sea; hot-dip galvanised steel with powder coating also performs well, as long as the coating stays intact. Mind the wind zone: the coast falls in the most severe zone, so have the column calculated for it.

How much lux belongs on a promenade?

As a guide value, expect 7.5 to 10 lux average with many pedestrians, less on a quiet quay. A lighting calculation on the actual column spacing gives certainty.

Did Lichtplein supply or install this project?

No. Lichtplein is a wholesaler and does not install lighting itself: installers and municipalities carry out the work. We share this project as an example of what custom work can mean and supply comparable luminaires and columns for new projects.
